# World Civilization & Macro-Geography Plan

Status: **macro topology phase resolved; civilization-detail planning authority. No later zone is auto-authorized by this document.**

Purpose: establish the world-scale geography, civilizational heartlands, separation barriers, settlement hierarchy, and travel expectations before additional edge-by-edge biome expansion causes unrelated regions to mesh together unnaturally.

## Macro topology lock — resolved

The first macro-geography pass is complete in `docs/WORLD_MACRO_TOPOLOGY.md`.

Locked decisions now include:

- no global square/hex world tessellation; the world uses irregular geographic envelopes, a canonical route graph, and local place coordinate systems;
- two principal surface landmasses (Central Continent + Southern Landmass), an Eastern Archipelago, plus Deep World and Pelagic layers;
- Ironspine as the northern massif and a distinct western mountain crescent around the Lethari approach;
- Great Mere as an open freshwater east-draining lake whose lower outflow reaches the Eastern Sea through a future brackish delta;
- Waymeet reserved on the northwestern/north-central interior plateau, with Headwater Vale as the first overland approach from Timbercross;
- Veyra separated from Emberwash by farther arid approaches plus a southern strait/inner sea;
- Miri placed beyond the eastern coast in an island/archipelago realm;
- Korren placed beyond/under Ironspine through controlled surface/deep gates;
- the 3-8-major-leg / 12-30+ hour ancestral-capital separation doctrine retained.

The macro planning hold is therefore lifted **as a geography blocker**, but no edge zone is auto-started. The post-lock recommended world-edge candidate is **Headwater Vale**.


## 1. Core design decision

The existing starting nations are **political states, not racial homelands**.

Current playable races:
- Human;
- Lethari;
- Miri;
- Veyra;
- Korren.

Current starting nations:
- Thornwall;
- Brasshaven;
- Mistmere.

Race and nation are separate character-creation choices. Preserve that.

Therefore:
- Thornwall, Brasshaven, and Mistmere should remain mixed-race metropolitan capitals;
- no existing capital should be retroactively declared "the elf city", "the dwarf city", etc.;
- racial/cultural homelands can exist elsewhere as older heartlands, ancestral realms, sovereign states, confederacies, enclaves, or diaspora centers;
- humans need not have one exclusive racial capital at all.

This distinction lets the existing compact world remain coherent while future racial heartlands gain stronger geographic identity and separation.

## 2. Current scale and what it implies

Canonical route distances/times currently make the three starting capitals a compact civilizational core:

- Thornwall -> Brasshaven by Crown-Forge road:
  - about 54,000 yalms;
  - about 21,600 route-seconds / 6 hours.
- Brasshaven -> Mistmere by Forge-Mere road:
  - about 45,000 yalms;
  - about 18,000 route-seconds / 5 hours.
- Thornwall -> Mistmere through the current road chain:
  - about 99,000 yalms;
  - about 11 route-hours.

That is suitable for three economically intertwined political capitals.

It is **too compact** to make each one the isolated ancestral seat of a fundamentally different people unless the setting intentionally wants those peoples to have lived almost on top of one another for centuries.

Recommended interpretation:

> Thornwall, Brasshaven, Mistmere, and the future Waymeet form the **Central Civilized Belt**: a dense mixed-race trade region on one principal landmass.

The racial heartlands should generally sit outside this belt.

## 3. Capital separation doctrine

Do not use distance alone.

A racial/civilizational heartland should normally be separated from another major heartland by:

1. **multiple macro-regions** of travel; and
2. **at least one meaningful barrier**.

Recommended scale for a major ancestral capital:
- normally 3-8 major travel legs from the Central Belt;
- roughly 12-30+ route-hours of ordinary travel before special delays;
- normally 120,000-300,000+ yalms of route distance if a continuous surface road exists;
- longer for oversea, subterranean, magical, or expedition routes.

These are planning targets, not hard runtime constants.

A barrier should change how travel works, not merely add another biome tile.

Barrier families:
- mountain wall / high pass;
- deep forest with limited known paths;
- desert / salt waste / water logistics;
- sea / strait / archipelago;
- major river or inland sea;
- magical concealment / shifting route;
- subterranean shaft / deep road / cavern chain;
- flooded or underwater access;
- political gate / treaty border;
- hostile environmental belt;
- sky route that bypasses otherwise long terrain.

A major homeland should ideally have **one primary mundane approach**, one expensive/specialist alternative, and large areas that cannot simply be crossed at any map edge.

## 4. Recommended macro-world structure

Use a **multi-layer world**, not a single uninterrupted quilt of adjacent biomes.

### A. Central Continent / Central Civilized Belt

Contains the currently authored world:
- Thornwall / Elderwood / Crownfields;
- Slatewater Foothills;
- Brasshaven / Redstone Reach;
- Coppergrass Steppe;
- Mistmere / Starfen / Great Mere;
- Ironspine southern approaches;
- future Waymeet and its marches.

Function:
- mixed-race political heartland;
- dense trade;
- easiest early-game movement;
- where diasporas, guilds, mixed neighborhoods, embassies, and cross-cultural professions are most normal.

The current capitals stay relatively close by world standards.

### B. Lethari ancestral heartland — recommended: hidden fertile valley realm

Working concept:
- a broad fertile upland valley or basin beyond deep old-growth and a mountain crescent;
- reached through one or two real passes and a partially magical forest route;
- not literally invisible, but difficult to find or enter without road knowledge, invitation, old maps, guides, or Lethari familiarity.

Suggested macro placement:
- west/northwest beyond Gloamwood and a farther mountain barrier;
- **not** directly adjacent to West Elderwood.

Character:
- ancient orchards, managed woodland, terraced farms, river meadows, oath-halls, old stone/wood architecture;
- woodland identity without making the entire homeland one endless forest biome.

Barrier:
- Gloamwood;
- ravines/mountain wall;
- warded or misleading approach roads.

Capital role:
- ancestral Lethari capital, not the only place Lethari live.

Secondary city slots:
- pass fortress/trade city;
- river/orchard regional city.

### C. Korren ancestral heartland — recommended: deep mountain/cavern realm

Working concept:
- a true subterranean or semi-subterranean civilization beneath a separate northern massif beyond the currently authored Ironspine frontier;
- cavern districts, vault roads, underground rivers, ventilation shafts, mushroom/fungus agriculture, quarry halls, deep foundries.

Important:
- **Deepvein Mine is not the Korren capital.**
- Lower Deepvein should remain part of Brasshaven's mining frontier.
- the Korren homeland should require several additional deep-road/highland regions before the ancestral citadel.

Barrier:
- Ironspine high country;
- controlled tunnel mouths;
- lifts/shafts/deep roads;
- cave hazards.

Capital role:
- large cavernous citadel with surface gates and deep districts.

Secondary city slots:
- surface gate-city;
- underground river/trade city;
- mining/forge satellite.

### D. Miri ancestral heartland — recommended: island/archipelago realm

Working concept:
- an island kingdom or compact scholarly archipelago beyond the eventual Starfen delta/brackish coast;
- reached primarily by ship;
- skyferry possible later but expensive and weather-dependent.

Why this fits:
- gives a clean physical barrier;
- allows very different magical/scholarly architecture without awkward biome adjacency;
- supports observatories, tideworks, gardens, libraries, artificer quarters, marine trade, and island agriculture.

Barrier:
- open sea;
- reefs/storm belts;
- navigational knowledge.

Capital role:
- island capital, perhaps built around a sheltered caldera, stepped harbor, or old magical observatory complex.

Secondary city slots:
- major port;
- outer-island academy/shipyard town.

### E. Veyra ancestral heartland — recommended: separate southern landmass or sea-separated peninsula

Working concept:
- a warmer southern realm of dry woodland, savanna, broken highlands, river corridors, and richer coastal belts;
- clan networks and mercantile routes can coexist with permanent large cities.

Recommended separation:
- do **not** simply continue Emberwash south until it touches the Veyra capital;
- use Emberwash/desert as one northern frontier, then an inland sea, strait, escarpment, or substantial uninhabited belt before the Veyra heartland.

Barrier options:
- desert + water logistics;
- strait / narrow sea;
- canyon escarpment;
- seasonal storm belt.

Capital role:
- major Veyra city with permanent markets, clan houses, caravan compounds, hunting guilds, foreign quarter, and court/council institutions.

Secondary city slots:
- coastal trade port;
- inland caravan/herding city.

### F. Merfolk / pelagic civilization — recommended non-surface realm

Working concept:
- a non-player or future-playable aquatic civilization on the continental shelf or reef systems beyond the eventual coast;
- not just an underwater dungeon.

Barrier:
- water itself;
- diving equipment, breathing magic, merfolk guides, pressure/depth limits, or specialized vessels.

Political access:
- initial diplomacy can occur at reef shrines, tidal embassies, floating markets, or surface gate-settlements;
- the true capital remains underwater.

Capital role:
- submerged city with vertical districts, reef agriculture, shell/stone architecture, current-based transport, and distinct resource logic.

This creates a genuine second world layer without requiring another continent.

### G. Optional dark-elven / deep-Lethari culture

Do **not** create a separate "dark elf race" yet.

First decide whether this concept is:
- a Lethari cultural branch;
- a magical/political faction;
- a genuinely separate ancestry;
- or unnecessary.

If retained, it should not share the same deep-city niche as the Korren without a strong reason.

Possible placement:
- a remote fungal cavern basin;
- under a different mountain chain;
- or a shadowed magical valley accessible through old under-roads.

This decision belongs in culture design before geography implementation.

## 5. Settlement hierarchy

Do not make every race one city.

Recommended hierarchy:

### World/ancestral capitals
Target eventually:
- 3 current mixed political capitals;
- 3-4 major racial ancestral capitals;
- 1 major neutral/cosmopolitan metropolis;
- optional aquatic/deep civilization capitals.

These should be rare and content-dense.

### Major regional cities
Each mature homeland should normally reserve room for:
- 1 capital;
- 1-2 secondary large cities;
- several towns/fortresses/ports/industrial settlements.

Secondary cities should have different functions, not be smaller copies of the capital:
- port;
- fortress;
- pilgrimage center;
- academy;
- mine/forge city;
- agricultural market;
- river junction;
- caravan hub;
- shipyard.

### Mixed-race cities

Mixed settlement should be normal, especially in:
- Thornwall;
- Brasshaven;
- Mistmere;
- Waymeet;
- ports;
- caravan junctions;
- frontier mining cities;
- treaty cities.

Race should influence neighborhoods, institutions, architecture, guild traditions, family networks, and migration history—not function as a hard citizenship lock.

## 6. Recommended role for Waymeet

Waymeet should become the setting's major **neutral cosmopolitan metropolis**, not another racial capital.

Possible identity:
- crossroads city;
- treaty city;
- free city;
- old imperial junction;
- merchant league seat.

It can plausibly contain:
- all playable races in large numbers;
- beastfolk citizens and embassies;
- foreign quarters;
- long-distance caravan and skyferry hubs;
- racial homeland embassies;
- neutral guild headquarters.

This gives the world one deliberately mixed international city while allowing ancestral capitals to retain stronger cultural identities.

Keep the existing Thornwall -> Waymeet skyferry as a bypass. A true overland route should cross multiple regions.

## 7. Beastfolk integration doctrine

Do not treat "beastfolk" as one civilization or as automatically hostile.

Species/ancestry and faction must be separate concepts.

For each beastfolk people, eventually decide:
- integrated citizens;
- diaspora minority;
- autonomous treaty community;
- sovereign polity;
- nomadic/confederated society;
- hostile faction;
- multiple factions with different relationships.

Rules:
1. a hostile goblin band does not make all goblins hostile;
2. beastfolk should appear as traders, artisans, laborers, guides, clergy, scholars, soldiers, and officials where appropriate;
3. major cities should eventually have at least some integrated non-player peoples where history supports it;
4. at least one substantial beastfolk-majority city or polity should exist;
5. diplomacy/reputation should be faction-specific rather than species-wide.

Possible placements:
- goblin caravan/engineering communities along Brasshaven trade;
- wetland or amphibious peoples around Starfen/coast;
- forest peoples in deeper western woods;
- steppe/clan peoples beyond Coppergrass;
- aquatic peoples overlapping the merfolk sphere.

Do not assign these permanently until the beastfolk roster is deliberately audited.

## 8. Continents and vertical layers

Recommended baseline:

### Surface geography
- **Central Continent**: current authored world + Lethari western/northwestern heartland + northern Ironspine approaches.
- **Southern Landmass or large separated peninsula**: Veyra heartland.
- **Eastern/outer archipelago**: Miri heartland.
- open ocean and island chains between/around them.

### Vertical geography
- **Deep World**: Korren realm, caves, deep roads, underground rivers, later deep-Lethari or other cultures if justified.
- **Pelagic World**: merfolk realm, reefs, shelf seas, drowned ruins.

This gives separation without requiring every civilization to be hundreds of empty wilderness zones apart.

## 9. Climate and biome planning before more zone authoring

The coarse macro framework required before further edge authoring is now established by `docs/WORLD_MACRO_TOPOLOGY.md`. Future zones such as Emberwash should continue to obey:

1. north/south orientation and rough latitude;
2. major mountain chains;
3. prevailing wet/dry sides of those mountains;
4. major river basins and drainage direction;
5. Great Mere's drainage/outflow;
6. coastlines and seas;
7. desert placement;
8. highland snow/cold zones;
9. tropical/subtropical limits if any;
10. magical climate anomalies;
11. major underground entrances;
12. continental shelf / reef zones.

Then place biomes **inside that framework** instead of discovering climate one adjacent zone at a time.

## 11. Resolved and still-open decisions

Resolved at macro level:

1. two principal surface landmasses plus an eastern archipelago;
2. western/eastern seas and a southern strait/inner-sea separation;
3. Ironspine northern massif + separate western crescent;
4. Great Mere as an open east-draining freshwater basin;
5. Lethari/Korren/Miri/Veyra homeland envelopes confirmed;
6. humans remain widespread rather than receiving an exclusive ancestral capital;
7. merfolk/pelagic civilization remains a valid major world layer;
8. the 3-8-leg / 12-30+ hour ancestral-capital separation target is retained;
9. route times remain literal fictional travel time under the current route system;
10. Waymeet is reserved as the neutral/cosmopolitan metropolis;
11. each major homeland has a primary barrier and ordinary/specialist approach concept;
12. capital/secondary-city slots are reserved before detailed wilderness fill.

Still open for later bounded culture/capital work:

- final political forms and proper names for ancestral states/capitals;
- exact coastlines and numerical regional extents;
- the first beastfolk peoples with recognized political communities;
- whether the dark-Lethari concept is a culture, separate ancestry, or discarded;
- exact specialist access gates and start-of-game availability for distant capitals.
