# Ecology Breadth Audit

This document records the canonical flora/fauna breadth criteria used during Phase 0.9 content expansion. It is a planning and regression aid, not a claim that the current three playable regions represent the final world's biome diversity.

## Audit result

Before this pass, canonical ecology was concentrated in temperate woodland, dry upland, cave, and fen starter environments. Animal representation was strongest in small herbivores, bats, a few ungulates, one boar-like family, limited insects/arthropods, one wetland bird, and one eel-like fish. Major functional/taxonomic niches such as mammalian predators, mustelids, reptiles, amphibians, pollinators, arachnids, crustaceans, mollusks, waterfowl, and scavenging birds were absent or poorly represented.

Raw resource coverage had useful timber, herbs, fiber, basic ores, clay, fish, berries, fungi, resin, kelp, and hunting materials, but weak coverage of staple grains/oils/fruits/salt/shellfish and luxury spices/dyes/perfumery/fine woods/gems.

The breadth pass raises the current canonical substrate to at least:

- 30 ecology families;
- 31 species;
- 29 persistent populations;
- 25 timed gathering sources;
- 6 explicitly tagged staple resources;
- 6 explicitly tagged luxury resources.

## Added family coverage

| Environment | Added families / niches |
| --- | --- |
| Elderwood forest and river | fox/canid, river otter/mustelid, owl, bee/pollinator, river turtle |
| Redstone upland and cave | marmot/rodent, lizard/reptile, scorpion/arachnid, cave salamander/amphibian, vulture/scavenger |
| Starfen marsh and ruin | frog/amphibian, reed crab/crustacean, waterfowl, freshwater mussel/mollusk, ruin spider/arachnid |

The new populations deliberately use previously underfilled places including East Elderwood, Timbercross Landing, North Redstone Reach, Deepvein Mine, East Starfen, and the Sunken Archive. Ecology expansion should continue to distribute life across the full place graph rather than repeatedly enriching only one anchor wilderness.

## Resource breadth added

Staples should support ordinary household, travel, preservation, food, feed, textile, construction, and workshop loops. Luxuries should create reasons for specialist gathering, trade, gifting, fine craft, collection, fashion, ritual, and regional prestige.

### Staple tranche

- Elderwood Hazel Nut — food and oilseed;
- Elderwood Crabapple — fruit and preserves;
- Ridge Millet — grain and fodder;
- Redstone Rock Salt — seasoning and preservation;
- Starfen Reedgrain — wetland grain;
- Fen Mussel — common aquatic protein.

### Luxury tranche

- Ghost Orchid — perfume/alchemical flower;
- Blackheart Heartwood — fine timber and furniture stock;
- Sun Crocus Stigma — spice, dye, and perfumery material;
- Redstone Fire Opal — gem/jewelry/arcane-focus material;
- Indigo Iris Petal — fine dye/textile input;
- Moonlotus Blossom — perfume, medicine, and collectible flower.

Resource breadth should not mean equal value or equal abundance. Staples should generally be renewable, comparatively common, and tied to repeatable livelihood loops. Luxury resources should usually be rarer, slower to regenerate, condition-bound, skill-gated, geographically narrow, or otherwise costly to obtain.

## Expansion rules

Future ecology passes should:

1. audit families and functional niches before adding more variants of already-common archetypes;
2. include predators, prey, scavengers, pollinators, decomposers, aquatic life, and sessile/invertebrate fauna where appropriate;
3. distinguish ambient species from executable combat encounters rather than forcing every animal into combat;
4. attach populations to believable habitats and use time/rarity/regeneration where useful;
5. keep resource provenance exact: source, place, action, and downstream sinks must agree;
6. maintain both subsistence/staple and luxury/specialty economies;
7. expand across all supported biomes instead of treating starter-region climates as the world template.

## High-priority future biome gaps

As geography expands, prioritize families and resources that cannot be represented well by the current starter regions:

- **coastal and marine:** schooling fish families, sharks/rays, cephalopods, marine crustaceans/mollusks, seabirds, marine mammals, coral/kelp/salt/pearls;
- **alpine and cold:** bears, lynx-like cats, pikas, eagles, cold-adapted herd animals, conifers, medicinal lichens, wool/fur and cold-weather food chains;
- **true desert:** snakes, beetles, desert birds, camelid/equid analogues, drought shrubs, dates/oil plants, incense resins, rare salts and gemstones;
- **tropical forest:** primates, large cats, parrots, snakes, diverse frogs/insects, fruit, spices, medicinal plants, rare hardwoods and dyes;
- **savanna/steppe:** bovids, equids, pack hunters/scavengers, ground birds, grasses, cereal/pulse resources and hides;
- **deep freshwater:** multiple fish families, aquatic insects, turtles/amphibians, shellfish, reeds and lake-specific food economies;
- **subterranean:** blind fish/crustaceans, cave insects, fungi, mineral salts, guano and unusual alchemical materials;
- **agricultural/settled ecology:** cattle, sheep/goats, poultry, working animals, cats/dogs, rodents/pests, cereals, pulses, oil crops, orchard fruit, flax-like fibers and managed apiaries.

These are breadth priorities, not a requirement to add every family immediately. New records should arrive in coherent regional packs with enough geography, livelihood, economy, quest, and production context to matter.
