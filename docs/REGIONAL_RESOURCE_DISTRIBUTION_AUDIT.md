# Regional Resource Distribution Audit

Status: **Data 51 regional-resilience audit implementation on main; final validation pending.**

## Audit question

For each established economic area, can local resources plus dependable nearby trade partners support ordinary food, tools, repairs, shelter, containers, cordage, and common production without requiring an implausibly unique or distant ingredient?

The target is **regional resilience, not autarky**.

A region does not need every ore, tree, grain, herb, or luxury good. It should normally be able to obtain the ordinary material families it lacks through one dependable trade basin, while retaining real regional specialties.

## Audit standard

For common production, an established economic basin should be able to cover:

- staple food and protein;
- wood, cane, stone, or clay for structures and implements;
- metal stock or a reliable source of imported metal stock;
- fiber, cordage, hide, or another practical binding material;
- fuel for hearths and ordinary industry;
- basic medicinal/herbal material;
- food preservation by at least one locally plausible path;
- a kitchen and/or light workshop at the main service locality where local processing is expected.

This does **not** require:
- local precious metal;
- every dye or luxury ingredient;
- every weapon/armor alloy;
- a full forge in every village;
- identical materials in every biome.

### Thornwall / Crownfields / Slatewater / Brasshaven

Reliable links:

- Crown-Forge Caravan Road;
- Slatewater Foothill Caravan.

This forms the western manufacturing basin.

- Slatewater -> timber, stone, clay, limestone, abrasives, resin, herbs;
- Redstone -> iron/copper/tin/lead/silver inputs, salt, flux minerals, glass sand.

### Brasshaven / Coppergrass / Mistmere

Reliable physical corridor:
- Forge-Mere Long Road.

Scheduled through-service:
- Forge-Mere Caravan between Brasshaven and Mistmere.

Coppergrass is currently a **transit wilderness**, not a staffed service settlement. The route physically crosses it, but the scheduled service does not currently expose Coppergrass as a boarding stop.

Core exchanges:
- Redstone -> metals, stone/mineral stock, salt;
- Coppergrass -> pulses, flax, dye roots, ornament stone, dry-steppe wood;
- Starfen -> grain, fish/shellfish, herbs, fibers, wetland wood/cane/clay.

### Mistmere / Great Mere

Reliable links:

Core exchanges:

- Great Mere -> fish, shellfish, roots/starches, rush fiber, pearl, shell lime.

### Brasshaven / Ironspine

Reliable physical corridor:

Core exchanges:
- Redstone -> iron/copper stock, salt, forge flux, larger manufactured goods;

## Area-by-area findings

### Elderwood / Thornwall

Local strengths:
- abundant differentiated timber;
- resin and glue feedstock;
- Barkboar hide;
- roots, mushrooms, nuts, fruit, sap;
- practical medicinal flora;
- full forge, workshop, woodshop, and tannery access in Thornwall.

Reliable imports:
- Crownfields grain/fiber;
- Slatewater stone/clay/abrasives;
- Redstone metals and salt.

Assessment: **robust**.

Intentional specialization:
- no local metal ore required;
- Thornwall is a manufacturing city supplied by the Crown-Forge corridor.

### Crownfields

Local strengths:
- rye, peas, apples;
- flax and hemp;
- orchard wood;
- new common brick clay;
- kitchen/workshop access at Crownfields Grange.

Reliable imports:
- Elderwood structural timber and hide;
- Slatewater stone and lime;
- Redstone metal and salt through Thornwall's freight network.

Assessment: **robust agricultural basin**.

Remediation:
- added Crownfields Brick Clay because an alluvial/drained farming lowland without ordinary brick clay was implausibly under-authored.

### Slatewater Foothills

Local strengths:
- spruce and cedar;
- white clay, slate, limestone, whetstone stone;
- pitch resin;
- herbs and berries.

Reliable imports:
- Redstone metal/salt from the east;
- Elderwood/Crownfields food, hide, fiber from the west.

Assessment: **robust corridor region**.

Remediation:
- Slatewater spruce can now be burned into ordinary charcoal, so Redstone metallurgy no longer depends specifically on Elderwood Crown Oak;
- the Waylodge hearth now exposes the kitchen/workshop already implied by its hot meals, drying racks, and repair role.

Intentional specialization:
- no local ore field was added;
- the region remains a timber/stone supplier between two manufacturing centers.

### Redstone Reach / Brasshaven

Local strengths:
- copper, iron, tin, calamine, lead, silver;
- clay, abrasives, alum shale, glass sand;
- rock salt;
- millet;
- Ridge Ibex hide;
- major forge/workshop infrastructure.

Reliable imports:
- Slatewater timber/resin/charcoal;
- Starfen/Coppergrass fiber and food;
- Slatewater/Starfen medicines;
- Ironspine gold and specialty minerals.

Assessment: **strong industrial center**.

Key correction:
- the original charcoal graph required Crown Oak from Elderwood even though Slatewater is Brasshaven's immediate timber-trade partner. Regional charcoal substitutions remove that artificial dependency.

### Coppergrass Steppe

Local strengths:
- groundpea;
- prairie flax;
- dye root;
- agate;
- new Thornwood scrub for fuel, handles, and rough construction.

Reliable imports:
- Redstone metal, salt, and stone;
- Starfen grain, fish, herbs, wetland fiber/wood.

Assessment: **adequate for a transit wilderness**.

Remediation:
- added Coppergrass Thornwood so the dry steppe is not literally woodless and can support ordinary camp fuel and rough implement stock.

Remaining infrastructure note:
- Forge-Mere caravans pass through Coppergrass but the scheduled service skips it as a boarding stop.
- If Coppergrass receives a permanent settlement, caravan-stop/workshop infrastructure should be added before treating it as a full manufacturing locality.

### Starfen / Mistmere

Local strengths:
- reedgrain;
- fish and mussels;
- herbs, berries, kelp;
- reed, nettle, and other fibers;
- Giant Cane;
- new Marsh Willow timber;
- new alluvial clay;
- kitchen and light-workshop support in Mistmere.

Reliable imports:
- Redstone metal and salt via Forge-Mere caravan;
- Coppergrass dryland fiber/food/dyes;
- Great Mere fishery and shell resources.

Assessment: **robust wetland craft/food basin**.

Remediation:
- added Marsh Willow because a large wetland/canal region with no ordinary working wood was implausible;
- added alluvial clay because canal banks and wetland sediment should support common ceramic/building clay;
- willow can now become charcoal for local light industry.

Intentional specialization:
- no local metal ore was added. Mistmere can import common metal stock reliably from Brasshaven rather than duplicating Redstone's geological identity.

### Great Mere / Merewatch

Local strengths:
- abundant fish, crayfish, mussels;
- cress and starchy roots;
- rush fiber;
- shell lime;
- pearl;
- kitchen and workshop at Merewatch.

Reliable imports:
- Starfen grain, herbs, willow/cane, fiber, clay;
- Redstone metals and salt through Mistmere.

Assessment: **robust freshwater basin after fallback correction**.

Remediation:
- added a reduced-yield dry-smoking route for Silver Perch that does not require Redstone rock salt.
- salted smoking remains the better-yield preservation route when trade salt is available.

This establishes the intended pattern:
- imported salt improves preservation;
- local smoking still prevents the fishery from dead-ending when salt supply is interrupted.

### Ironspine Highlands

Local strengths:
- Snowhorn game and hide;
- bear hide/fat;
- lynx fur;
- alpine greens/remedies;
- lodestone, quartz, gold;
- new Stonepine timber;
- new pass building stone;
- kitchen/workshop at High-Pass Watch.

Reliable imports:
- Redstone common metals, salt, forge flux, and heavy manufactured goods.

Assessment: **adequate highland frontier economy**.

Remediation:
- the Lower Pass description already contained Stonepine but only cones were harvestable; timber is now canonical;
- a mountainous pass with no ordinary building stone was also implausibly under-authored, so common pass stone is now recoverable;
- Stonepine can become local charcoal.

Intentional specialization:
- fine textiles can remain imported;
- hides, fur, willow bark, and local timber provide practical frontier substitutes for many bindings and weatherproof goods.

## What should remain regionally scarce

Do **not** flatten these distinctions merely to make every area self-contained:

- silver: Deepvein / Redstone specialty;
- gold: Ironspine specialty;
- copper/tin/brass/bronze industrial chain: Redstone-centered;
- Cloud Quartz/lodestone: Ironspine specialty;
- premium hardwood and bow wood: Elderwood specialty;
- mast/spar cedar/spruce stock: Slatewater specialty;
- hemp/linen agricultural textile base: Crownfields specialty;
- wetland fibers/cane: Starfen and Great Mere specialty;
- pearls: Great Mere specialty;
- luxury dyes/perfumes/gems: remain regional.

The fallback rule is:
**ordinary substitutes should exist; premium outcomes may still require specialty trade.**

Examples:
- ordinary local or imported iron/copper tool before precious-metal ornament;
- rough Thornwood or Willow component before premium Ash/Yew;
- dry-smoked fish before salted/pickled export-grade preservation;
- local clay vessel before fine Slatewater white-slip ware.

## Remaining audit risks

### 1. Finished tools are still under-authored as production outputs

The resource basin is now much healthier, but many shop tools are still vendor definitions rather than craft outputs.

Next material-culture packet should consume the new resilient substrate rather than inventing unique inputs.

Priority conversions:
- Field Knife;
- Prospector Pick;
- Woodsman Hatchet;
- Digging Spade;
- Reed Sickle;
- Marsh Fishing Rod;
- Ash Staff;
- Maple Wand;
- Iron Buckler;
- Brass Ring;
- Bronze arms/armor;
- basic leather goods.

### 2. Recipe alternatives need to be used deliberately

Where an ordinary item can be made from multiple regional materials, prefer separate recipes producing equivalent or region-flavored outcomes rather than forcing one canonical tree/ore/herb into every economy.

### 3. Coppergrass is not yet a staffed economic node

It has enough resources to support camps and passing caravans, but no permanent locality/workshop authority. Keep this distinction until a settlement is intentionally authored.

### 4. Husbandry remains a real missing source family

Wool, milk, eggs, honey, manure, and other managed-animal outputs still require a deliberate husbandry authority. Do not fake them as flora.

## Audit rule for future zones

Before a new zone is considered economically integrated, check:

1. What can inhabitants eat locally?
2. What can they burn for heat/cooking/industry?
3. What supplies handles, poles, boards, baskets, or structural members?
4. What supplies cordage/textile/bindings?
5. What supplies common tool metal, locally or by dependable trade?
6. What supplies stone/clay/lime or a functional construction substitute?
7. What supplies medicine and preservation?
8. Which nearby route/service makes imported essentials reliable?
9. Which premium materials remain intentionally unavailable?
10. Can the main service locality actually expose the workstations implied by its fiction?
