# Coppergrass Steppe — Zone Profile

## Canonical identity

- **Stable ID:** `coppergrass-steppe`
- **Display name:** Coppergrass Steppe
- **Map:** `map-coppergrass-steppe`
- **Type:** wilderness
- **Region:** Coppergrass Steppe
- **Nation:** none / neutral corridor
- **Danger level:** 2
- **Primary route:** Forge-Mere Long Road
- **Geographic role:** transitional interior steppe between Redstone Reach and Starfen

Coppergrass Steppe is not a fourth starter nation or a disguised extension of Brasshaven or Mistmere. It is a broad neutral travel landscape whose importance comes from geography: the easiest overland corridor between the forge country and the eastern wetlands crosses it.

## Placement

The existing world topology establishes Brasshaven's Iron Quay on the eastern side of Brasshaven and Mistmere's Reedport on the western side of Mistmere. The Forge-Mere Long Road already connected those two points across 45,000 yalms and carried both upland-weather and fen-weather hazards without an intervening landscape.

Coppergrass Steppe occupies that missing middle.

Conceptual west-to-east profile:

```text
Redstone Reach / Brasshaven
        |
        | stony fans, quarry dust, thin upland soils
        v
+---------------------------+
|    COPPERGRASS STEPPE     |
| western gravel grassland  |
| rolling loess steppe      |
| seasonal basins/swales    |
| wetter eastern channels   |
+---------------------------+
        |
        | sedge swales, seasonal water, softer ground
        v
West Starfen / Mistmere
```

The Forge-Mere Long Road is split into two equal geographic legs:

- **Brasshaven Iron Quay → Coppergrass Steppe:** 22,500 yalms / 9,000 seconds;
- **Coppergrass Steppe → Mistmere Reedport:** 22,500 yalms / 9,000 seconds.

The full established corridor remains 45,000 yalms / 18,000 seconds.

## Biome

**Temperate semi-arid steppe with seasonal drainage basins.**

Coppergrass is drier than Starfen but less mineral-bare than Redstone Reach. Rainfall is strongly seasonal and uneven. Most water arrives as spring storms or short heavy fronts rolling eastward from the Redstone uplands.

The defining vegetation is copper-brown bunchgrass that cures to a reddish metallic sheen in late summer. Deeper loess pockets support pulses, flax-like herbs, dye roots, and taller seed grasses. Low eastern swales remain green longer and gradually transition toward sedges and reed margins.

### Why this biome belongs here

The adjacent landscapes imply a descending moisture and elevation gradient:

1. Redstone contributes exposed rock, gravel fans, mineral-rich runoff, heat, and wind.
2. The middle basin accumulates finer windblown soil and supports grassland rather than bare upland.
3. Seasonal runoff spreads through shallow channels and closed depressions.
4. Farther east, the water table rises and drainage becomes persistent enough to support Starfen marsh-grassland.

This provides a continuous geographic explanation for the existing `upland-weather → fen-weather` route transition.

## Terrain structure

### Western stony fans

The western edge is the hardest, driest ground. Gravel washed out of Redstone gullies forms broad fans interrupted by shallow cuts and exposed agate-bearing cobbles.

Characteristics:

- sparse bunchgrass;
- excellent wagon footing when dry;
- severe crosswinds;
- dust and grassfire exposure;
- Windglass Agate deposits.

### Central loess grassland

The central steppe consists of rolling, fine-soiled grassland with long sightlines and shallow ridges.

Characteristics:

- densest grazer habitat;
- groundpea and prairie flax patches;
- tall seedgrass;
- burrow systems;
- major predator/prey interactions;
- primary route alignment.

### Seasonal basins

Shallow depressions collect stormwater for days or weeks after rain.

Characteristics:

- richer soils;
- dense insect emergence;
- Tallcrest Bustard feeding grounds;
- temporary grazing concentration;
- periodic mud that slows carts.

### Eastern swales

The eastern third lowers toward Starfen.

Characteristics:

- deeper grass;
- wetter soils;
- sedge intrusion;
- longer-lived stream channels;
- flood hazard after storms;
- early signs of fen ecology.

## Climate and weather

Coppergrass has a strongly continental feel despite lying on a major trade road.

- **Spring:** windy, green, storm-prone; seasonal basins fill and migratory birds become common.
- **Summer:** hot afternoons, dry grass, dust haze, grassfire risk.
- **Autumn:** clear skies, strong night cooling, seed harvest, animal movement toward eastern water.
- **Winter:** cold winds, hard ground, sparse surface water, excellent long-road visibility.

Major route hazards are:

- crosswind;
- grassfire;
- roadside raiders;
- seasonal flooding toward the east;
- deteriorating fen-weather near Mistmere.

## Fauna profile

### Dun Courser

- **Family:** Steppe Courser
- **Role:** primary medium/large grazer
- **Habitat:** open grassland and broad central flats
- **Behavior:** wary herd animal
- **Ecological function:** converts grassland productivity into mobile prey biomass and concentrates predators around water and migration paths

Dun Coursers are the visual signature animal of the zone. Herds are usually seen at distance before they are approached.

### Slateback Wolf

- **Family:** Steppe Wolf
- **Role:** pack predator
- **Habitat:** dry swales, broken grass, basin rims
- **Behavior:** territorial pack hunter, most active late in the day
- **Ecological relationship:** follows courser movement and scavenges caravan-road kills when hunting is poor

### Tallcrest Bustard

- **Family:** Bustard
- **Role:** large ground bird / omnivorous forager
- **Habitat:** open grassland and seasonal basins
- **Behavior:** wary daytime flocks
- **Ecological function:** consumes seeds, shoots, and abundant insects after rain

### Copperwing Locust

- **Family:** Locust
- **Role:** high-abundance herbivorous insect
- **Habitat:** seedgrass and warm open steppe
- **Behavior:** daytime swarms
- **Ecological function:** major pulse prey item and occasional pressure on forage availability

### Stormglass Kite

- **Family:** Steppe Kite
- **Role:** aerial predator
- **Habitat:** wind ridges and open flats
- **Behavior:** solitary daytime hunter
- **Ecological relationship:** strongly associated with locust emergences and small-animal movement after storms

## Flora and resource profile

Coppergrass deliberately adds resource classes that remain useful at both household and specialist-economy scales.

### Staples

#### Coppergrass Groundpea

- pulse / protein crop analogue;
- wild-foraged from deep loess pockets;
- ordinary food and processing input;
- common enough to support repeat livelihood use.

#### Prairie Flax

- fiber and oilseed;
- harvested from seasonal-basin margins;
- supports textile, oil, cordage, and general crafting directions;
- common renewable material rather than a prestige plant.

### Luxuries

#### Crimson Madder Root

- concentrated red dye source;
- favors deeper dry-swale soils;
- lower abundance and slower regeneration;
- specialist textile and decorative trade good.

#### Windglass Agate

- banded agate exposed in western gravel fans;
- gathered from mineral-rich seasonal washes;
- ornament, jewelry, collectible, and fine-craft material;
- rarer and skill-gated compared with ordinary steppe resources.

## Economic role

Coppergrass is economically important because it sits between two very different production centers.

### Westbound movement

Toward Brasshaven:

- flax fiber;
- dye root;
- food pulses;
- agate for lapidary and fine metalwork;
- marsh goods transiting from Mistmere.

### Eastbound movement

Toward Mistmere:

- Brasshaven tools and worked metal;
- mineral goods;
- caravan equipment;
- steppe pulses and fiber;
- western imports continuing through the corridor.

The zone therefore supports more than extraction. It can eventually host:

- seasonal drover camps;
- road wardens;
- caravan repair clearings;
- temporary dye-buying camps;
- livestock or courser-related husbandry;
- raider pressure;
- toll or jurisdiction disputes between neighboring powers;
- storm/flood route closures.

## Human geography

Coppergrass has no major sovereign settlement in the initial profile.

That absence is intentional.

Permanent habitation is limited by:

- unreliable surface water;
- fire exposure;
- long distances;
- seasonal mud in eastern basins;
- political ambiguity between stronger neighboring powers.

Likely future human sites include small wells, caravan shelters, herder camps, boundary stones, abandoned roadworks, and one or more neutral waystations. These should be added only when NPC/service/quest content is ready to make them functional.

## Exploration character

The zone should feel visually and mechanically different from all three starter wildernesses.

Players should experience:

- long sightlines rather than forest concealment;
- moving animal groups rather than mostly fixed encounter pockets;
- weather visible before it arrives;
- route decisions shaped by fire, wind, and temporary water;
- resource patches associated with soil and drainage rather than generic gather nodes;
- a clear sense of traveling from stone country toward wet country.

## Future expansion hooks

High-value follow-on content for Coppergrass should favor systems that emerge naturally from the profile:

1. migrating herd state and predator pressure;
2. seasonal fire/flood route conditions;
3. caravan encounters and temporary camps;
4. pulse/oil/fiber production chains;
5. dye and lapidary luxury production;
6. neutral road-warden NPCs and a functional waystation;
7. one deeper grassland or badland subzone before adding another entirely new biome.

Coppergrass should remain a transition region with its own identity, not become a miscellaneous dumping ground for creatures or resources that do not fit elsewhere.
